What was applied: Auphonic Speech Isolation (denoisemethod=speech_isolation, amount 100), adaptive leveler, loudness normalised to −16 LUFS with −1.5 dBTP ceiling, 48 kHz WAV. Nothing else was changed — no EQ, no gate, no generative resynthesis.
How the sections were chosen: the full 2-hour recording was scanned in 30-second windows measuring the noise floor between words (10th percentile of 100 ms RMS). The noisy section is the worst-scoring speech-bearing window; the clean section is among the best. Neither was picked by ear.
Level-matched, deliberately: Auphonic normalises its output to −16 LUFS, which made the processed clips ~20 dB louder than the raw ones. Louder always wins a listening test, so the BEFORE clips here have pure linear gain applied (+19.2 dB and +21.8 dB) to sit at the same loudness. Gain lifts signal and noise together, so what you hear is the denoising, not a volume trick.
Reading the numbers: "noise floor" is what you hear in the gaps between words — lower is cleaner. "Speech level" should stay roughly the same: if it moved a lot, the voice itself was altered.
