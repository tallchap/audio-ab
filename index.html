Why Auphonic alone was not enough, and what the de-bleed step does

The artifacts in Richard's pauses are not noise — they are Liron's voice, coming out of Richard's speakers into Richard's mic, 150–450 ms late (network round-trip, and it jitters). Riverside's noise gate opens on the louder syllables and you get short speech-shaped blips. Speech Isolation is built to keep speech, so it keeps them.

debleed.py TARGET REFERENCE OUT finds them programmatically across the whole track, no ear involved. It splits Richard's gated track into ungated "islands", and for every island checks whether Liron is talking just before it and whether its waveform cross-correlates with Liron's track over the delay range. An island is removed when it is short and quiet while Liron talks (gate chatter), when its waveform matches Liron's (echo), when it stays 10 dB+ under Richard's speech level for as long as Liron talks (gate stuck open), or — your rule — when it sits within 1 s of a confirmed artifact cluster while Liron is still talking and is not clearly Richard's own sustained speech. Removed ranges are muted with 10 ms fades; everything else is bit-identical.

Whole-track result on richard-liron-riverside-audio-hipfilter.wav (2h00m): 2,335 islands, 1,212 removed, 482 s total — 1,055 gate chatter, 83 quiet-under-Liron, 25 stuck-open stretches (three of them ~59 s long, at 41:43, 49:28 and 93:08), 44 next-to-cluster, 5 by waveform match. 26 of the removed islands peak above −16 dBFS and are the only ones that could plausibly be a real "yeah" from Richard under Liron's speech; they are listed with timestamps in richard_removed.csv (every removed range, with the reason) so any can be exempted. Script: debleed.py.

What was applied: Richard: debleed.py (above), then Auphonic. All: Auphonic Speech Isolation (denoisemethod=speech_isolation, amount 100), adaptive leveler, loudness normalised to −16 LUFS with −1.5 dBTP ceiling, 48 kHz WAV. Nothing else was changed — no EQ, no gate, no generative resynthesis.
How the sections were chosen: each full track was scanned in 30-second windows. Because Riverside tracks are gated (true silence between words is −100 dB), the metric is the level of the ungated 100 ms frames that sit 18 dB or more under the speech level — the room tone, hiss and breath that leaks through the gate. Each speaker's section is the worst-scoring window of their own track. None were picked by ear.
Level-matched, deliberately: Auphonic normalises its output to −16 LUFS, which made the processed clips ~20 dB louder than the raw ones. Louder always wins a listening test, so the BEFORE clips here have pure linear gain applied (@@GAINS@@) to sit at the same loudness. Gain lifts signal and noise together, so what you hear is the denoising, not a volume trick.
Reading the numbers: "excess sound in pauses" is what you hear in the gaps between words — lower is cleaner. "Speech level" should stay roughly the same: if it moved a lot, the voice itself was altered.
